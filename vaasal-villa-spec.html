Vaasal Villa Management System

Blueprint for one integrated hospitality platform: public booking website, property management, channel distribution, tour operator portal, restaurant POS, housekeeping, key-card access, staff time & attendance, and analytics, all on one central database.

Audience architects, senior engineers, QA, PMStatus baseline for estimationDate 28 Sep 2026

01System overview

What the platform is, who uses it, and the architectural stance everything else follows from.

Vision

Vaasal Villa runs one or more villa properties with an on-site restaurant and ancillary services. Today these functions typically live in disconnected tools: an OTA extranet, a spreadsheet calendar, a standalone POS, paper housekeeping sheets and a separate door-lock program. The platform replaces them with one system of record so that every guest, villa, booking, charge, card and staff action is captured once and visible everywhere it matters.

Core value proposition

  • Zero double bookings. A single availability ledger governs every channel: website, walk-in, phone, admin, tour operators and OTAs.
  • One guest bill. Room nights, restaurant, laundry, spa, transport and other services post to one guest folio and settle in one checkout.
  • Operational flow without hand-offs. Checkout automatically revokes the key card, flags the villa dirty, creates the housekeeping task, and returns the villa to sale once inspected.
  • Direct revenue. A premium, fast, mobile-first website with secure payment and social/WhatsApp reach reduces OTA commission dependency.
  • Management visibility. Real-time dashboards for occupancy, revenue, receivables, POS, inventory, staff attendance and access activity.

Architectural stance: modular monolith on one database

The system is built as a modular monolith: one deployable backend with strictly bounded internal modules (Booking, Villa, Folio, POS, Housekeeping, Access, Staff, Operator, Channel, CMS, Reporting, Identity). Each module owns its tables, exposes a service interface and publishes domain events; no module writes another module's tables directly. All surfaces talk to the backend over a versioned REST API plus WebSocket channels for real-time screens.

Why not microservices? The dominant correctness problems here (availability, folio balance, card validity) are transactional and cross-module: a checkout must close a folio, revoke a card and dirty a villa atomically. One ACID database makes those guarantees cheap. Traffic at villa scale is modest, and a small team pays heavily for distributed operations. Module boundaries plus an internal event bus keep the option open to extract a module (e.g. Channel sync, Reporting) into its own service later, without a rewrite.

Booking lifecycle

Enquiry→Hold→Tentative→Confirmed→Checked-in→Checked-out·CancelledNo-showExpired
  • BK-01 Central availability calendar: grid of villas × dates, colour-coded by status and source; drag to move/extend with conflict checks; blocks for maintenance and owner use.
  • BK-02 Double-booking prevention: inventory is a per-villa, per-night ledger with a uniqueness constraint (villa_id, stay_date). Holds, bookings and blocks all write to it inside one transaction; a conflicting write fails, never overwrites.
  • BK-03 Holds with TTL for in-progress web/portal checkouts; expired holds are released by a scheduled job and on read.
  • BK-04 Rate management: rate plans (e.g. Room Only, B&B, Non-refundable), base rates, seasonal rates, day-of-week modifiers, length-of-stay rules, min/max stay, closed-to-arrival/departure, extra-guest charges, child policy.
  • BK-05 Cancellation policies attached to rate plans; automatic penalty calculation and refund proposal on cancel.
  • BK-06 Modifications: date change, villa change, guest count change, with re-pricing and audit trail of before/after.
  • BK-07 Group bookings: parent booking with multiple villa lines, shared or split folios.
  • BK-08 Guest profiles: deduplicated by email/phone, stay history, preferences, VIP/blacklist flags, marketing consent.
  • BK-09 Channel sync (see Integration §10): availability, rates and restrictions pushed to the channel manager on every inventory change; OTA bookings, modifications and cancellations pulled in via webhook, idempotent on OTA reference.
  • BK-10 Overbooking guard for OTA race conditions: if an inbound OTA booking conflicts, it is accepted into a conflict queue, the manager is alerted immediately, and relocation workflow is offered. The system never silently drops an OTA booking.
  • BK-11 Payment links: generate a hosted payment link for a booking balance; delivered by email/WhatsApp.
  • BK-12 Notifications on new booking, modification, cancellation, payment received, arrival tomorrow.

VLVilla management

  • VL-01 Villa CRUD with soft delete (a villa with history is archived, never hard-deleted).
  • VL-02 Villa types (e.g. Garden Villa, Pool Villa, Family Villa) carrying default capacity, bed configuration, base rate and facility set; individual villas inherit and may override.
  • VL-03 Facilities catalogue (private pool, AC, kitchenette, sea view…) with icons, filterable on the website.
  • VL-04 Pricing: base, seasonal, rate-plan-specific; managed jointly with BK-04.
  • VL-05 Media: ordered photos (auto-generated WebP/AVIF variants and responsive sizes), videos (hosted on a video service, embedded), cover image, alt text required.
  • VL-06 Status dimensions kept separate, because they answer different questions:
    • Occupancy: Vacant, Occupied, Due out, Due in.
    • Housekeeping: Dirty, Cleaning, Inspection, Clean, Ready.
    • Maintenance: OK, Issue reported, Out of order (removed from inventory), Out of service (sellable with warning).
    • Booking: derived from the calendar for any date.
  • VL-07 Occupancy tracking per villa and date range; feeds reporting.
  • VL-08 Lock mapping: each villa records its door lock ID(s) in the lock vendor’s system (used by the Key Card module).

KCKey card control

RFID key cardA contactless card (commonly MIFARE Classic/DESFire) that the vendor’s encoder writes with an access grant for specific locks and a validity window. The card UID is its factory serial number, used by the platform to track physical cards.
  • KC-01 Card inventory: register cards by UID (manual entry or read on encoder), card type, status (Available, Issued, Lost, Blocked, Damaged, Retired).
  • KC-02 Assignment: card ↔ guest ↔ booking ↔ villa, with validity from check-in time to scheduled checkout time (+ configurable grace).
  • KC-03 Issue / duplicate: encode first card (new key, invalidates previous) or duplicate (additional card, same grant) through the vendor encoder.
  • KC-04 Extend: late checkout or stay extension re-encodes or updates the grant online (for networked locks).
  • KC-05 Lost card: mark lost → issue new card as “new key” so the old card is rejected at the lock; online locks are updated immediately, offline locks on next new-key card use.
  • KC-06 Revoke / block: at checkout or on demand; card returns to inventory or is blocked.
  • KC-07 Access permissions for staff cards: master (all villas), zone (a block of villas), housekeeping (time-windowed), maintenance, emergency; linked to staff profile.
  • KC-08 Access history: import lock audit trails (online locks in near real time; offline locks when read by the vendor’s audit device) into access_events.
  • KC-09 Audit log of every platform action on cards: who issued, when, for which booking, from which encoder.
  • KC-10 Hardware abstraction: a LockProvider interface with one adapter per vendor. See Integration §10.
Constraint to plan around: Most lock vendors require their own lock-server software and a licensed PMS interface on the property LAN. Card encoding cannot be done purely from the cloud; the on-premise Lock Bridge service is mandatory. The specific vendor must be chosen (or the installed one identified) before Phase 5 estimation is final.

FDCheck-in & check-out

Check-in workflow

Search booking (ref, name, phone, OTA ref, arrivals list) → Verify guest identity → capture ID/passport (scan/photo upload, number, nationality, expiry) → Register all adult guests (local guest-registration requirements) → Assign / confirm villa (must be Ready; system warns otherwise) → Collect deposit / balance / security deposit (cash, card, pre-authorisation) → Issue key card(s) via encoder → sign registration card (on-screen signature) → Status: booking CHECKED-IN, villa OCCUPIED, folio OPEN, POS can charge to villa

During stay

  • FD-01 Post additional charges (minibar, laundry, spa, transport, damages) to the folio; see Guest service charges.
  • FD-02 Room move with folio carry-over and key re-issue.
  • FD-03 Stay extension / early departure with re-pricing and inventory update.

Check-out workflow

Open folio → verify all POS checks closed / posted, minibar checked → Review charges, apply adjustments (with permission + reason) → Calculate balance (charges − payments − deposits) → take payment / refund / transfer to city ledger (operator or company account) → Generate final invoice + receipt (print / PDF / email) → Revoke key cards → folio CLOSED → booking CHECKED-OUT → Villa: OCCUPIED → VACANT + DIRTY → housekeeping task auto-created
  • FD-04 Checkout cannot complete with a non-zero balance unless transferred to an approved city-ledger account.
  • FD-05 Express checkout: pre-authorised card, invoice emailed.
  • FD-06 Arrivals, in-house and departures lists with filters and printable versions.
  • FD-07 Night audit: a daily close that posts room charges for each occupied night, flags no-shows, rolls the business date and snapshots KPIs.
FolioThe running account of a guest stay: every charge and every payment, in date order. A booking may have several folios (e.g. room charges billed to a tour operator, extras paid by the guest). City ledger: receivable accounts for parties billed after departure, such as tour operators or companies.

HKHousekeeping

Dirty→Cleaning→Inspection→Clean→Ready(inspection fail →Cleaning)
  • HK-01 Status board: all villas with occupancy + housekeeping + maintenance status, priority (due-in today first), assigned staff.
  • HK-02 Task generation: automatic on checkout (departure clean), daily for occupied villas (stay-over service, configurable), manual ad-hoc tasks.
  • HK-03 Assignment by supervisor (drag-and-drop) or auto-distribution by workload/zone.
  • HK-04 Start/end timestamps captured from the phone app; duration tracked vs. standard time per villa type.
  • HK-05 Checklists: templates per task type and villa type; each item tick-able, photo evidence optional/required per item.
  • HK-06 Linen & towel requirements: par levels per villa type; quantities issued/returned per task; aggregated for laundry.
  • HK-07 Lost & found: item, where found, photo, finder, storage location, guest match, return/dispose record.
  • HK-08 Maintenance reporting: issue category, severity, photos; creates a maintenance ticket; severity “blocking” sets villa Out of Order automatically.
  • HK-09 Inspection: cleaner requests inspection; supervisor approves (→ Clean/Ready) or rejects with notes (→ Cleaning).
  • HK-10 Auto-availability: when a villa reaches Ready and has no maintenance block, it is flagged sellable for same-day arrivals and the front desk is notified.
  • HK-11 Offline tolerance: the app queues status changes when Wi-Fi drops and syncs on reconnect, with server-side conflict resolution by timestamp.

Guest folio integration

Waiter selects “Charge to villa” → searches in-house villa / guest → POS validates: booking CHECKED-IN, folio OPEN, guest allowed to charge, credit limit not exceeded → Guest signs (on-screen) → POS invoice status CHARGED_TO_ROOM → Folio module posts line: dept=Restaurant, ref=POS invoice no., net, tax, service → Visible immediately at front desk; settled at checkout with room charges

The same posting interface (FolioService.post()) is used by every department (restaurant, bar, laundry, spa, transport, minibar), so the final invoice combines all services into one folio.

04Data model & database schema

One relational database (MySQL 8 / MariaDB 10.11+ or PostgreSQL 16) with module-owned table prefixes. Every business table carries property_id from day one to make multi-property a configuration, not a migration.

Conventions

  • Primary keys: BIGINT UNSIGNED auto-increment internally; public references use ULIDs or human codes (VV-26-0412) so IDs are not enumerable.
  • Money: DECIMAL(14,2) plus currency CHAR(3). Never floats. Exchange rate captured on the transaction.
  • Time: DATETIME in UTC; stay dates are DATE in the property’s local calendar.
  • Standard columns: created_at, updated_at, created_by, updated_by; soft delete (deleted_at) for master data only. Financial rows are never deleted or updated in place; corrections are reversing entries.
  • Enums as lookup tables or constrained VARCHAR with application-level validation, so new statuses do not need schema changes.
  • Optimistic locking (version INT) on bookings, folios and orders edited concurrently.

Why a per-night inventory row? A date-range overlap check (arrival < x AND departure > y) is correct but races under concurrent writes unless the whole villa row is locked. 05API architecture

One versioned REST API (/api/v1) serves every first-party client. JSON, resource-oriented, OpenAPI 3.1 documented, contract-tested.

Conventions

  • Auth: session cookie (HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax) + CSRF token for browser apps; short-lived bearer tokens (OAuth2 client credentials) for devices, Lock Bridge and partner integrations.
  • Idempotency: Idempotency-Key header required on payment, booking creation, POS order submission and folio posting.
  • Pagination (cursor), filtering (filter[status]=confirmed), sorting, sparse fields.
  • Errors: RFC 9457 problem+json with machine-readable code (e.g. INVENTORY_CONFLICT, FOLIO_CLOSED).
  • Concurrency: ETag/If-Match on mutable aggregates.
  • Rate limits per client and per IP; stricter on public and auth endpoints.
  • Real-time: WebSocket channels per property (property.{id}.kds.{station}, .hk, .frontdesk, .notifications.{user}).

08Security specifications

Authentication

  • Email + password; passwords hashed with Argon2id (bcrypt cost ≥ 12 fallback); minimum 10 characters, checked against breached-password list.
  • Optional TOTP MFA; mandatory for Owner, Admin, Manager, Accountant.
  • Forgot password: single-use, 60-minute, hashed token; response identical whether the email exists or not.
  • Change password requires current password; all other sessions revoked on change.
  • POS quick-switch: 4–6 digit PIN, only on registered devices, only after device-level login.

Sessions

  • Server-side sessions, HttpOnly + Secure + SameSite cookies; ID regenerated on login.
  • Idle timeout: 30 min back office, 15 min for finance roles; absolute 12 h. POS devices lock to PIN screen after 2 min idle.
  • Explicit logout invalidates the server session; admins can kill sessions per user.

Failed-login protection

  • Progressive delay after 3 failures; account lock 15 min after 5; IP rate limit; CAPTCHA on public forms and after repeated failures.
  • All attempts written to login_logs with IP and user agent; alert on anomalous patterns.

Authorisation

  • Deny-by-default policy checks on every endpoint, enforced server-side; UI hiding is cosmetic only.
  • Row-level scoping by property_id and, for operators, tour_operator_id via global query scopes.

Application security

  • CSRF tokens on all state-changing browser requests.
  • Input validation with form-request classes; output encoding by default; parameterised queries only.
  • Content Security Policy, HSTS, X-Frame-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy headers.
  • File uploads: MIME + magic-byte validation, size limits, re-encoding of images, randomised names, stored outside web root; ID/passport scans in a private bucket with signed short-lived URLs; malware scan (ClamAV).
  • Dependency and container scanning in CI.

Data protection

  • TLS 1.2+ everywhere; encryption at rest for DB and storage; field-level encryption for document numbers and MFA secrets.
  • No card data stored: hosted payment fields / redirect and tokenisation keep the platform in PCI DSS SAQ-A scope.
  • Personal data retention policy (e.g. ID scans purged N days after checkout unless legally required); guest data export and erasure on request (GDPR / applicable local data-protection law).
  • Secrets in environment config / secret manager, never in the repository; per-environment API keys; key rotation procedure.
  • Third-party calls: timeouts, retries with backoff, circuit breakers, verified webhook signatures, and errors surfaced to staff without leaking internals.

Audit

  • Append-only audit log for financial, access-card, permission and data-export actions; periodic hash-chaining to detect tampering.

09UI/UX requirements

Design principles

  • Public site: premium and calm. Photography leads; generous whitespace; restrained palette derived from the property’s landscape; serif display with a clean sans body.
  • Operational apps: fast and unambiguous. Dense but legible; status by colour and label/icon; primary action always in the same place; large touch targets (≥ 44 px, ≥ 56 px on POS).
  • One design system of tokens (colour, type, spacing, radius, elevation, motion) shared across all surfaces.

Light and dark mode

  • Both themes designed intentionally (not inverted), each meeting AA contrast.
  • Default follows OS prefers-color-scheme; user toggle overrides; preference saved to the user profile (signed-in) or local storage (public site).
  • Theme applied before first paint (inline script) to avoid flash; transition of 200 ms on colour properties only.
  • KDS offers a high-contrast dark theme by default for kitchen conditions.

Responsive behaviour

  • Mobile-first; breakpoints at 640 / 768 / 1024 / 1280 px.
  • Back-office tables collapse to cards on narrow screens; calendar switches to agenda view on phones.
  • Mobile navigation: bottom bar for housekeeping app; off-canvas menu with focus trap on website.

Media guidelines

  • Hero image/video backgrounds: video muted, < 3 MB, poster frame, disabled on save-data and small screens.
  • Images: responsive srcset, AVIF/WebP with JPEG fallback, lazy loading below the fold, blurred placeholders, explicit dimensions to prevent layout shift.
  • Galleries, sliders and lightbox with keyboard and swipe support.
  • Performance budget: ≤ 170 KB JS (gzipped) on public pages; animation libraries loaded only where used.

Animation guidelines (WEB-18)

Motion follows the place: calm water and slow light. It should feel unhurried, purposeful and quiet. The eight principles below apply to every public page.

  1. Purpose first. Motion must explain something: a change of state, where content came from, or what is loading. Otherwise it is removed.
  2. Durations. 150–250 ms for feedback (hover, press, toggle); 300–400 ms for panels and modals; 600–900 ms for scroll reveal. Only ambient hero motion may run longer.
  3. Easing. ease-out / cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1) when entering and ease-in when leaving. No bounce or overshoot.
  4. Cheap properties only. Animate only transform, opacity and SVG stroke-dashoffset. Never animate layout properties.
  5. Visible at rest. Content must never depend on JavaScript to appear. Reveal effects hide content only after the script confirms it can reveal it, and counters render their final value in the HTML.
  6. Reduced motion. Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, all animation and transitions are disabled, sliders stop auto-advancing and reveals show immediately.
  7. One thing at a time. Stagger siblings by at most 100 ms, with at most one ambient animation in view.
  8. Back office stays still. Staff, POS and KDS screens use feedback transitions only (< 250 ms).

10Integration points

Every external dependency sits behind an internal interface with a provider adapter, so vendors can be swapped by configuration.

OTA & channel management

Channel managerA certified intermediary (e.g. SiteMinder, Channex, Cloudbeds, RateGain) that keeps availability and rates in sync across many OTAs through one API. OTA: Online Travel Agency such as Booking.com, Agoda, Expedia or Airbnb.
  • Recommended: integrate one channel manager API rather than each OTA directly. Direct connectivity to Booking.com, Agoda, Expedia and Airbnb requires connectivity-partner certification for each, which is slow and not guaranteed for a single property.
  • Outbound (ARI): availability, rates, restrictions per villa type/rate plan per date, pushed via outbox on change, plus a nightly full resync.
  • Inbound: new/modified/cancelled reservations via webhook; acknowledged only after the booking is committed; idempotent on (channel, external_ref).
  • Mapping UI: platform villa types and rate plans ↔ channel room/rate codes.
  • Fallback: iCal import/export for channels without API access (e.g. some Airbnb listings), with clearly-labelled lower sync guarantees.

Payment gateways

  • PaymentGateway interface: createIntent, capture, refund, createPaymentLink, verifyWebhook.
  • Primary international gateway (e.g. Stripe) plus a regional gateway for local cards/currency where the property operates (e.g. PayHere, Razorpay or the local bank’s IPG); selection per currency.
  • 3-D Secure, pre-authorisation for security deposits, tokenised card-on-file for guarantees.
  • Card-present POS: integrated terminal SDK where available; otherwise manual reference entry with daily reconciliation report.

RFID locks & encoders

  • LockProvider interface: encodeNew, encodeDuplicate, readCard, revoke, extend, fetchAuditTrail.
  • Candidate adapters: ASSA ABLOY VingCard (Visionline / Vostio), dormakaba (Ambiance), Salto (Space / KS), Onity, and budget OEM systems exposing a DLL SDK.
  • Topology: cloud → job queue → Lock Bridge (property LAN) → vendor lock server / USB encoder. Bridge authenticates with client credentials, connects outbound only, and reports each job’s result. Receptionist sees “Place card on encoder” and a live result.
  • Failure handling: encoder timeout or error returns a clear message and leaves the card unassigned; no silent partial states.

Messaging & social

  • WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API): template messages for confirmations, pre-arrival, payment links and checkout invoices (opt-in required); inbound messages to a shared inbox with booking linking. Click-to-chat link on website.
  • Facebook & Instagram (Meta Graph API): display feed on website, Messenger/Instagram DM link, Meta Pixel / Conversions API for booking attribution (consent-gated).
  • TikTok: embed videos, TikTok Pixel (consent-gated).
  • YouTube: video hosting and embedding for villa tours (privacy-enhanced mode).
  • Email: transactional provider (Amazon SES, Postmark or Mailgun) with SPF, DKIM, DMARC; bounce webhooks.
  • SMS fallback provider for OTP and critical alerts.

Location, weather, media, analytics

  • Maps: Google Maps Platform or Mapbox for location map and directions; static map image on documents.
  • Weather: OpenWeather or similar, cached server-side (15 min) and shown on website and front-desk dashboard.
  • Image/video optimisation: Cloudflare Images/Stream or equivalent, or self-hosted pipeline.
  • Web analytics: GA4 or privacy-friendly alternative; cookie consent manager governs all tags.
  • Accounting (future): export journals to Xero / QuickBooks / local accounting software via revenue account mapping.

Sequenced by dependency, not by importance: every later module posts to the booking, villa and folio core, so that core ships first. Durations assume a team of about 4–5 developers, 1 designer and 1 QA, and are indicative until estimation.

P0wk 1–4

Foundations

Repo, CI/CD, environments, design system and tokens (light/dark), identity (login, password flows, sessions, MFA, failed-login protection), RBAC engine, audit/login logs, property model, notifications framework, file storage, PDF service.
P1wk 5–12

Core PMS

Villa management, rates & seasons, inventory ledger, availability calendar, bookings (admin, walk-in, phone), guest profiles, folio & payments, check-in/out, service charges, night audit, core documents (confirmation, invoice, receipt).
P2wk 10–18

Website & online booking

CMS, all public pages, booking engine with holds, payment gateway, emails/vouchers, WhatsApp click-to-chat and social embeds, SEO, performance and accessibility pass. Starts in parallel once P1 inventory and rates APIs are stable.
P3wk 15–22

Housekeeping & staff

Housekeeping PWA, checklists, inspection, linen, lost & found, maintenance tickets; staff profiles, rosters, time clock, attendance calculations, leave.
P4wk 19–30

Restaurant POS

Outlets, tables, menu & modifiers, orders, KOT/KDS, billing, payments, shifts & drawer, charge-to-villa folio integration, inventory & recipes, suppliers, POS reports, offline mode.
P5wk 26–32

Key card control

Vendor selection confirmed, Lock Bridge, encoder integration, card lifecycle, staff cards, access history import. Includes on-site testing with real locks.
P6wk 28–34

Tour operator module

Registration, portal, contracts & allotments, group bookings, rooming lists, deposits, commission, statements, operator documents.
P7wk 32–38

Channel management

Channel manager integration, mapping UI, ARI push, reservation webhooks, conflict queue, iCal fallback, certification with provider.
P8wk 36–42

Analytics, WhatsApp API & polish

Dashboards and full report catalogue, scheduled reports, WhatsApp Business templates, weather/maps, social pixels with consent, animation polish.
P9wk 42–46

Hardening & launch

Security review and penetration test, load test, data migration from existing tools, staff training, parallel run, go-live, hypercare.
Milestone gates: end of P1, internal staff can run the property on the new PMS · end of P2, direct bookings go live · end of P4, restaurant runs on the POS with folio posting · end of P7, OTAs are synchronised and the old calendar is retired. After go-live, roadmap modules (§12) are scheduled by business value.
